In short: Quantum Arcade is a free course in English that teaches quantum computing with interactive mini-games and a state-vector simulator written from scratch. You start from waves and the classical Fourier transform, build qubits, gates and entanglement, and arrive at Grover, QFT, Quantum Phase Estimation and Shor. To play you need a free account (first name, last name, email): progress is saved on the server and follows you anywhere, the AI tutor answers your questions, and the final exam — graded by the server — issues a PDF certificate with a publicly verifiable code.

How it works

1 · Touch first

Every concept arrives as a mini-game: sliders, arrows to drag, circuits to assemble. First you move and get it wrong, then you read the explanation. That is not a gimmick: learning this way lasts longer.

2 · All the maths is here

This is not a "no formulas" course: they are all here, from the simplest to the heaviest. But every symbol is a button: touch it and it tells you what it does, with the real numbers you are moving in the game.

3 · It ends with inventing

The last level is a workshop: you stack quantum blocks like bricks, try them on open challenges and go after your own record. There you do not repeat: you invent.

🔁 Flash review

A few questions, asked again at the right moment (after 1 day, then 3, then 7, then 21). It is the combination of active recall and spaced repetition: of all the study techniques measured by research, these are the two that work best.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know maths already?

No. Part 0 covers negative numbers, fractions and percentages, coordinates, degrees and probability: it is written for someone who has just finished middle school. Sine, cosine, complex numbers and e^{iθ} get built inside the course, starting from a point turning on a circle.

Is it a real simulator?

Yes: a state-vector simulator written from scratch (complex amplitudes, controlled gates, probabilistic measurement, entanglement). The QFT circuit you assemble is checked by automated tests: it reproduces the Fourier matrix exactly.

Do I have to register?

Yes, and it is free: first name, last name, email and password. It is not about collecting data — it is because progress is saved on the server (you find it again on every device) and because the final certificate carries your details and a code anyone can verify. If the state lived only in the browser, anyone could edit it and the certificate would be worth nothing.

How long does it take?

Each level is 10–20 minutes played without rushing; the whole path is a long afternoon. But the final workshop can keep you busy for weeks, because there the solutions are not written down anywhere.

I am a teacher: can I use it in class?

If you teach in a state or state-recognised school, at a university, in a library, in a public body or in a non-profit association: yes, freely and without asking for anything. The same goes for after-school clubs and free study groups. You can download everything, modify it, translate it or install it on your own server: the code is public and contributions are welcome.
